Artist statement

I learned this country one season at a time. A street I thought I knew in July was a different street in January. The light arrived from a new angle, the colour drained or flared, the distances felt longer or shorter. Nothing had moved except the year.

These photographs were taken in the summer-fall transition when change is most visible. I am an engineer by training and I have studied how light behaves from a physics perspective; however, how we see light is moody, messy, it has a strong bias. I shot these photographs on Harman Red and Harman Azure because of what those films do with light: where the exposure is generous they turn the world red and magenta, and the greens stay where they were. For photography light is the medium so I chose a film stock that deliberately pushes the boundaries of what is "fake" and what is "real". The pictures are ordered by colour, from the reds of the overexposed sky through orange and amber to the greens the film leaves alone, and each is matted in its own hue to complement the scene.

I hold a deep reverence for the seasons. I like trees because they show us temperance, patience and fortitude, the attributes it takes to live in this climate. As an immigrant I see a familiar place twice, once as it is and once against the memory of somewhere else. The seasons do the same to everyone. They make the ordinary briefly strange, and then ordinary again.

Bio

Daniel E. Mosquera P. came to Canada at the age of nine, in 2004, and has lived in Edmonton for most of his life. He holds a bachelor's degree in materials engineering from the University of Alberta (2017) and has been making photographs for more than ten years.

His photographs return to the familiar places of his city as the seasons turn, and ask how perspective changes with the light that falls on them.
