I came to Canada as an immigrant, and I learned the country one season at a time. The places I passed every day refused to stay the same. A street I thought I knew in July was a different street in January. The light arrived from a new angle, the colour drained or flared, the distances felt longer or shorter. Nothing had moved except the year.

This body of work follows that experience. I return to familiar places and photograph them as the seasons turn, paying attention to what the light does to them rather than to what they are. The same corner, the same stretch of water, the same row of trees: each one is a fixed point that the year passes through, and each photograph is a record of one moment in that passage.

I have been making photographs for more than ten years, and the longer I do it the more I think the subject is perspective itself. Not only the position of the camera, but the position of the person holding it. An immigrant sees a familiar place twice, once as it is and once against the memory of somewhere else. The seasons do something similar to everyone. They make the ordinary briefly strange, and then they make it ordinary again.

These photographs are an invitation to look at a known place as if arriving for the first time, and to notice how much of what we call a place is really the light that happens to be falling on it.
