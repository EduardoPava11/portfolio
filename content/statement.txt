I came to Canada at nine, in 2004, and have lived in Edmonton most of my life. I learned this country one season at a time. A street I thought I knew in July was a different street in January. The light arrived from a new angle, the colour drained or flared, the distances felt longer or shorter. Nothing had moved except the year.

These photographs were made in autumn, when that change is most visible. I am an engineer by training and I know how light behaves, so I chose a material that shows it. I shot them on Harman Red and Harman Azure because of what those films do with light: where the exposure is generous they turn the world red and magenta, and the greens stay where they were. The change of season, of colour and of light is one event, and I wanted a film that would show it instead of correcting it away.

I return to the same places as the year turns and pay attention to what the light does to them rather than to what they are. Light is the medium, and used deliberately it pushes the boundaries of our limited vision. The pictures are ordered by colour, from the reds of the overexposed sky through orange and amber to the greens the film leaves alone, and each is matted in its own hue.

I hold a deep reverence for the seasons. I like trees because they show us temperance, patience and fortitude, the attributes it takes to live in this climate. An immigrant sees a familiar place twice, once as it is and once against the memory of somewhere else. The seasons do the same to everyone. They make the ordinary briefly strange, and then ordinary again.
