I came to Canada at the age of nine, in 2004, and I have lived in Edmonton for most of my life. I learned this country one season at a time. The places I passed every day refused to stay the same. A street I thought I knew in July was a different street in January. The light arrived from a new angle, the colour drained or flared, the distances felt longer or shorter. Nothing had moved except the year.

These photographs were made in autumn, the season in which that change is most visible. I shot them on Harman Red and Harman Azure because of what those films do with light: where the exposure is generous they turn the world red and magenta, and the greens stay where they were. The change of season, the change of colour and the change in the light are one event, and I wanted a material that would show that event instead of correcting it away. The film does to a familiar street what autumn does to it.

I return to the same places as the year turns and pay attention to what the light does to them rather than to what they are. The pictures are ordered by colour, from the reds of the overexposed sky through orange and amber to the greens the film leaves alone.

I hold a deep reverence for the seasons. I like trees because they show us temperance, patience and fortitude, the attributes it takes to live in this climate. An immigrant sees a familiar place twice, once as it is and once against the memory of somewhere else. The seasons do something similar to everyone. They make the ordinary briefly strange, and then they make it ordinary again.
